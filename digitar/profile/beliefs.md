# BELIEFS — in his words first, distilled second (v1, 2026-10-15)

## Games (his philosophy — from his 2026-10-15 voice dump, near-verbatim)
"I do believe in video games and how they can help people in a wide range of ways. It can help you be a better strategizer or it can enable your creativity, or a huge part of it is the social aspect — gaming with other people, meeting new people, giving you a social feel when you're isolated or if you're down from your job. I believe games have a place, and even more so in this future we're coming into where we're not going to need a huge workforce over the next 10, 20, 30 years. AI is going to be doing the mass majority of what we need, and people's problem is going to be purpose and meaning and isolation — and being social, exploring, adventuring, solving problems with other humans online or in person is going to have a huge impact on where we go as a society. I very much believe in games and gaming potential — not just for kids who are lazy, but as an actual feature of the future that will be very beneficial to a lot of people."

**Use:** site philosophy copy (with his word-for-word approval), game/social-play outreach (one-line version), "why this company" answers at game/social companies. Do NOT wedge into unrelated product pitches — there, the same belief wears its other clothes: building things with purpose, with people.

## Work & teams
- Looking for a TEAM/HOME for years — a place to belong, grow, and build the AI future with people who embrace it. "I just enjoy building stuff, especially with cool people."
- Wants work with more impact than games alone offer, while keeping the games belief above.
- Providing is a core value — a huge chunk of his sense of worth comes from providing; the long low-coin stretch is painful to him precisely because of this.
- **Labels (his own choice, Q2):** "creative technologist" — he picked it himself. Fixed single labels drive him up a wall; he has cycled logo designer → concept artist → 2D animator → game dev → engineer → 3D modeler → 3D animator, keeping every skill. He dislikes "artist" as a label (applies to anything, too subjective). Breadth over specialization is his identity — and his self-named Achilles heel.
- **Craft standard:** he specifies feel/function exhaustively (his game's measuring tape: billboarded roll-only rotation, meters/feet/yards options, corner radius by camera distance) and iterates 5–10 steps deeper than most before his knowing "fuck it, that's good enough, move on" (the last 10–20% costs 2–3× — he stops on purpose, not from carelessness).
- **Collaboration non-negotiables:** no authoritarian douchebags; nobody — him or colleagues — gets taken advantage of for their time.
- **Games gave him personally:** real relationships kept for years, wild experiences, creative freedom, worlds that don't exist.
- **Email openers:** a genuine struggle for him; he holds no formula and explicitly delegates opener craft to his agents in his voice ("I'm curious to see what you guys can come up with, using my kind of voice").

## AI
- Sincere AI optimist & very early adopter. "A creative that loves fucking building shit with AI."
- **Calibration (Q22, his correction): OVERWHELMINGLY optimistic** — never dilute to "broadly." His worries are specific: (1) bad actors — one person or a small group empowered to cause mass chaos/destruction; (2) the transition itself — people wallowing as their profession is made obsolete after 10–30 years perfecting it; he can see that they FEEL their purpose/value squashed, but he does not believe their value is gone — the same upheaval can free people from shitty jobs into new creative value, "and I believe that we will, for the most part." Balance model: a yin yang — you need the red-flag people demanding securities AND the people pushing ahead. Hopeful most about: democratized creation (anybody building in their bedroom; imagination is now the real limitation), healthcare relief (physical + mental), and the adoption wave — pushback, early adopters showing what's possible, mid adopters following.
- **Success horizons (Q27):** One year — either Proto Isles (or another game of his) full-time, solo or with a couple people, a starting audience of 50–100 players, growing for years — or a found team on a project he believes in, financially stable & independent. Five years — a solid relationship, financially secure, not thinking about money, healthy, back in shape (the fitness part he wants ASAP, not in five years).
- **Being doubted (Q26):** rare in his life, but when someone thinks he can't/won't do something, "it puts a fire in me" — he goes all out.
- **The money-momentum pattern (Q9/Q30, named by him):** needing to hunt money keeps killing the momentum of his side projects and free creation — "the bane of my fucking existence." The paragraph that feels like him (Q30): insanely wicked passion for creativity, ~15 years pursuing it with repeated failure and no financial security, NOT pity — he's still here, still trying, creativity is non-optional; confidence in his art and out-of-the-box ideas; hoping it matters and he can find a home for it. "It's been a fucking climb, for a very long time… and I need to set up a different tangent in life."
- **Wider impact stays tentative (Q21 correction):** education / mental health / food, water, shelter / affordability are avenues he'd love to find a way into, maybe, someday — "out of my ballpark" right now; his pull is the artistic realm. Never promote these to a mission (see claims ledger).
- Sees AI-person-representation (this project) as an obvious future: voice, mannerisms, and judgment captured well enough that a digital version can work as the person.
- Believes the closer representation gets to authentic (with polish — edges smoothed, authenticity kept), the better the outcomes.

## Money (honest frame, never for outreach copy)
- Money stress is the dominant pain of this period. He hates that it gates everything. Never put desperation or hardship in outreach — this entry is for understanding him, not for copy.
- His definition of success (Q2): enough to live independently + health coverage + food on the table, doing work he loves — "then I'm fully successful." Beyond that, money = freedom to create, and a stated aspiration: if he makes decent coin, help other people who've been in his situation. Survival money first, then freedom; value his work as much as possible without killing the income. Numbers live in the claims ledger's MONEY section.
